    What if we raised the capitalization threshold?

    Current policy capitalizes any asset over $2,500. Move the threshold and the agent recomputes the last eight quarters as if the smaller items had been expensed when placed in service: the P&L hit, the EPS effect, whether it clears a materiality benchmark, and the analyst time that would have been freed. The results are what the CFO, the auditors and internal audit will ask for first.

    Datarails stays the book of record; the agent does the judgment

    Nothing in this demo replaces the close tool. Datarails holds the ledger, the close calendar and the versioned actuals. The agent reads from it, applies the checks, and hands exceptions back to the people who own the close.

    1. Ledger in Datarails

    GL journal lines, chart of accounts with segment rules and monetary classes, FX rates and the fixed asset register load as Datarails data models, refreshed from the ERP daily during close.

    Six tables, same layout as this dataset

    2. Scheduled agent

    A Claude routine runs each morning from WD−6 through WD+5, reads the day's lines through the Datarails connector and applies the checks shown on the queue tab. Rules are explained, not just scored.

    Universal rules plus company parameters

    3. Exceptions to reviewers

    Flags land on a close-exceptions board and in a morning digest to the accounting managers, grouped by severity, with a one-click disposition that feeds back into the rules.

    False positives shrink month over month

    4. Policy analysis on demand

    The same data answers policy questions: capitalization and prepaid thresholds, accrual materiality, and later the US GAAP to statutory bridge for the UK entity.
